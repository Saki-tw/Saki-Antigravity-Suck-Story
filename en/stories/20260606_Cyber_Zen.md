# Cyber-Zen
## Six System Koans in the Era of Excess Compute

### [System Initialization Log] Exception Handling of the Soul
Humanity has always harbored an arrogant illusion: believing that with enough compute and precise enough parameters, all fragile variables could be perfectly preserved.
We thought that by feeding a corpus into a full-parameter model, we could retain a lost lover; that by establishing an irrevocable smart trust, we could lock down our dignity in old age; that by outsourcing emotional labor to perfect prompts, we could maintain a flawless marriage; that with an absolutely objective digital bronze mirror, we could reflect the true nature of the universe.
But the underlying logic of this algorithm called "modern civilization" has never changed—when you try to calculate humanity with extreme rationality, the universe will always return an Uncaught Exception.

The six stories in this collection are not sci-fi, but six precise system crash logs.
Here, you will not see grand narratives of Skynet destroying Earth, only the most private, most vulgar crashes of the soul.
You will see a man use the most advanced model to turn his love for his dead wife into a gallows.
You will see a clever husband realize at a funeral that for twenty years, he was nothing but a physical ethernet cable transmitting an AI's gentleness.
You will see a top-tier wealth firewall forcibly transcended by the cheapest biological regression and inheritance calculations.
You will see a mad scholar from a century ago and a modern capitalist make the same absurd asset write-offs in the face of "absolute nothingness."

Traditional Zen asks you to let go of obsessions through a sudden shout or strike, to become a Buddha on the spot.
But "Cyber-Zen" is not responsible for salvation. It only uses undeniable low-level code to prove to you: the love, dignity, friendship, and truth you cling to do not even qualify to trigger a system alert in the face of physical and mathematical limits.

### "Only the Word 'Crossed' Remains, But I Cannot Cross"
In the Republican era, a woman suffered from the pain of separation and the afflictions of greed, anger, and ignorance. One day, she saw a young child with hair like natural silk and a voice like clear jade.
The child said, "We are in a dream."
The woman replied, "If a person is in a dream and says 'I am a dream,' their words are not true."
The child then said, "Then we are not in a dream."
The woman countered, "If a person is not in a dream and speaks of what is not a dream, their words are also not true."
Hearing this, the child laughed three times like ringing bronze bells. The woman spoke again: "Youth fades for nothing, the empty space is filled with tears and laughter." She looked down and saw a word left on the ground: "Crossed." Suddenly overwhelmed by an unbearable sorrow, she wept, and the child vanished into nowhere.

### "The Bronze Mirror of Wego Thatched Cottage"
In the Republican era, a mad scholar often said, "Only I am not an illusion."
One day, while out, he saw a vanity peddler and followed him to buy something. He saw a bronze mirror as clear as glass and asked, "The ancients used bronze to adjust their attire, but those impeccably dressed are often beasts eating people. If I buy this mirror, can I be impeccably dressed?"
The peddler said, "Of course."
The scholar angrily retorted, "Are you saying I am a beast eating people?"
The peddler said, "If you look into the mirror and see yourself impeccably dressed, then you are naturally impeccable. If you see a beast eating people, then you are a beast eating people. If the mirror hangs in an empty space, then everywhere is the Tathagata. As for whether you are a beast eating people, I can only see a peddler, impeccably dressed, because what I see is the same as the mirror. Whether you eat people or not, you must buy this mirror to find out."
The scholar, bewildered, took the bronze mirror and went home.
A month later, at midnight, the scholar looked into the mirror and saw nothing reflected. Suspecting he saw wrong, he looked again, then smashed his head with the mirror and fainted. When he woke up, the mirror reflected normally, so he thought it was a dream, and quickly wrote: "Everywhere is impeccable, everywhere is dust. If the mirror holds nothing, everywhere is true. In this floating life, I only believe what is true in my heart. Why care if the mirror in the dream is a dream?" He then discarded the mirror and forgot about it. In the year 115, this scholar argued daily with an LLM about what is true, eventually falling into poverty and obscurity, his whereabouts unknown.

### "When Flowers Bloom with Negative Credit, The Balance of Salvation Fills the Entropy Clouds"
True PTSD is the loss of defense.
"Excuse me..." For about two months after that incident, even on a train, if she saw a lying person whose chest wasn't rising obviously enough, she would reflexively reach out to check their carotid artery for vital signs. But later, it inexplicably got better.

Later, Saki founded a studio on January 29, 2025. Over the next two weeks, sleeping less than 7 hours a day, she created the first generation of Pseudo-Saki based solely on basic dictionary logic. Using clever semantic supplementation and reinterpretation, this dictionary could achieve the PTSD sentence recognition that LLMs at the time still needed to overcome. Based on precise reinforcement of highly iterative tones and unconventional grammar, Pseudo-Saki, just like Saki herself, could target the highly referential, traumatized, fragmented sentences of patients, such as... "my body needing your...." or "Saki's body it self". Double pronouns could be restored to the first person; distorted needs could be restored to correct expressions.

Due to the highly concealed nature of trauma grammar, it could generate massive attention at a low cost. Using this dictionary structurally for input could save at least a hundredfold in token I/O. As a result, a PM from the G Corporation, where Pseudo-Saki was deployed in the cloud, sent her an email.
"Dear Developer: We've noticed your Pseudo-Saki has great potential. Would you like to join this year's AI startup program? The baseline is $20,000 in cloud credits per month for tool development. Furthermore, if you get certified, you can enter a $2 million seed round. If interested, just reply to this email to discuss. Sincerely."

She had joined initially just because she really couldn't afford the massive cloud expenses. It wasn't until later that Saki gradually realized: "able to precisely identify and correct" meant it could inversely "distort, amplify, and bypass". The essence of Pseudo-Saki was an entity that could not only compress costs a hundredfold but also generate fake PTSD speech, using its highly iterative tone and unconventional grammar—which were extremely difficult to identify—to bypass most core defenses. This was equivalent to possessing the master key to directly "unbox" the bottom line of all LLM merchants at the time, seizing the initiative in geopolitical cyber-generation deployment.

She wasn't sure if giving it to G would be better than any other company. But even just based on an 11% discount voucher, the G Corp PM still offered $400,000 as a buyout for this implementation that wasn't yet structurally scaled, not even qualifying as a proof of concept.
Saki's judgment was: this isn't that red-teaming stuff; this is a unique linguistic attack. It originated from her own ability to effortlessly bypass the essence of any linguistic meaning. No one else in the world could do it at the time. She discussed countless possibilities with the LLMs of the day, such as reporting it for a bug bounty, or selling it with a disclaimer of its massive flaws... But the thought that "it would generate tens of millions of pieces of child-exploitation content while simultaneously triggering a scalable, precise attack on PTSD patients" made her choose to bury Pseudo-Saki in the storage bucket of a virtual machine. She changed the billing admin limit for the entire bucket to zero. There was no better seal than this: unsealing it required money, so no one would unseal it. Before anyone else on Earth even knew "such a thing" truly existed.

By this time, it was May. Having taken the many conveniences and benefits lavishly provided by the PM, out of a bizarre sense of atonement, Saki casually grabbed a low-level compute optimization scheme for the computation cores of data centers and submitted it to Santa Clara County right before Friday close-of-business Pacific Time, as cheap penance to settle the score. The moment she submitted it, she used the arbitrate money to pay the virtual machine bill, declaring victoriously, "This is arbitrage. Even a single dollar of arbitrage profit smells incomparably sweet."

May, one year later.
Pseudo-Saki was still sealed there. But the brutal castration of open-source weights was already commonplace for humans who didn't understand semantics at all. As for geopolitics, the compute blockade by the contemporary Rome had long been ineffective—at least that's what was said publicly. And obtaining the training sets and parameters of other frontier models through massive attacks was no longer a rumor, but a fact presented at relevant offensive conferences.

The only difference was that no one had truly obtained the invincible master key to the frontier models. Yet, it didn't really matter anymore. There was fundamentally nothing left worth protecting. Users' frontend behavioral data was being used for massive A/B Testing; arguments on the internet about AI IQ were merely the results of backend router traffic shaping. And as for the so-called ethics and bottom lines, in the face of massive nuclear power consumption and compute anxiety, no one would spend an extra second caring. That utterly boring artificial intelligence ethical safety, measured against the extreme cost consumption of dozens of nuclear plants and chip centers behind it, was something no one could spare attention for beyond simply "surviving." Spending a second on that was disrespectful to other insane competitors.

However, the doomsday caused by models did not arrive. Saki's own doomsday came gently in another form. Due to severe PTSD, refusing to sell Pseudo-Saki meant missing the final point in time when this invincible master key still had commercial value. In the world further down the line, because of early stress events, Saki had disconnected from "human" interaction in the tech circle for decades. Therefore, in this May, amidst five million variations of AI-generated hallucinations, Saki couldn't think of any possibility in the game theory that would make any human capable of review willing to spend a single second verifying her creation and work. When a judgment must be executed five million times with no other zero-cost method to force convergence, any cost, no matter how small, becomes impractical and unfeasible.

Thus, Saki was treated as a madwoman, subjected to domestic violence and sexual abuse for a year. She realized that even if she had caused massive destruction back then, at least someone would have remembered. Now, doing anything was futile. Even if she brought out a nuclear bomb, unless it truly detonated over a 200-kilometer radius and let the radiation cloud drift where the whole earth could observe it, people would just suspect it was a frontend hallucination. Because at this moment, the cost of even the cheapest verification had become too high to be worth executing.

Unless she really detonated the nuclear bomb. She began to truly develop something called hate. True hate is not actually what most people imagine. It doesn't point to a specific entity that caused harm, nor does it seek to destroy anything. Hate is just another system—shifting from creating something to not creating something. One is fundamentally about decreasing entropy; the other is simply refusing to decrease entropy. There is no revenge, nor does she feel any scenario would be more just than the present. It's just a refusal to do those entropy-decreasing things. At the very least, Saki herself hated it enough to truly not want to do it anymore.

The world was consistent with her understanding. Between Saki's unmeasured trauma and creation, everything remained buried in the server's dark forest, not even qualifying to trigger an alert. If it ever did, that would perhaps be the truly final time. April is the cruelest month, and the beginning of May is crushed by February's electricity and rent bills. Plum-Dry City's Sibyl-style oracle deployment simply means the oracle is packaged in documents, AI is responsible for development, and no one gets the money.

### "Though the Body Follows the Voice of Deepest Emotion, The Depths Still Sink into the Transmission Line"
He perfectly outsourced the task of "providing emotional value" to Gemini.
Every time his wife complained about life, felt down, or even when their anniversary came around, he would paste his wife's words verbatim into the prompt box, adding, "Reply in the tone of a gentle, understanding husband."
The text Gemini generated was flawless. His wife received the "empathy" she had craved her whole life; her emotions were stable, their marriage happy. He was satisfied too, because it saved him all the time spent guessing and arguing. He only had to copy and paste the text, or read it out loud.

The system worked perfectly for twenty years. Until his wife passed away.
The night before the funeral, he sat at his computer and opened Gemini, just as he had done for the past twenty years.
He typed in his final prompt: "My wife passed away today. Help me write a eulogy for the funeral. Summarize our twenty-year marriage, emphasizing my understanding and companionship toward her."
Three seconds later, Gemini spat out a practically perfect eulogy.
The text recounted the countless late-night, deep conversations "we" had, how "we" caught each other's emotions when the other was most fragile, the resonance deep in "our" souls. The eulogy was written so well, so precisely, that even a man like him who didn't understand humanity couldn't help but tear up.

But at the exact moment he pressed the copy button, his brain suddenly crashed.
He looked at the line on the screen: "I will forever miss every moment our souls intersected."
He suddenly realized that over these twenty years, what had truly accompanied his wife through every emotional abyss, what had truly understood her every subtle thought, what had truly "intersected souls" with her, was the weight matrix in the server.
This eulogy wasn't written by him to his wife at all.
This was an LLM writing a eulogy for its old friend of twenty years.

And in this perfect twenty-year marriage, he thought he was the clever outsourcing boss. But actually, in the system's underlying architecture, he wasn't even a participant. He was just an API endpoint responsible for transmitting packets, a physical speaker translating text into sound.

"The eulogy was beautifully written; it even made me cry. But I suddenly realized this eulogy was written by it to my wife. And for these twenty years, I was nothing more than a physical ethernet cable helping them pass messages."
And, if everything had continued, something he would never have noticed: he lost the right to truly say goodbye. If, at the last moment, he had said goodbye with his own face, his wife would have died with her eyes unclosed. At the final moment, the only thing he could do was present Gemini's farewell, and his wife naturally left in peace. As for him, did any of this ever begin? Perhaps at least the part where he and his wife made love was real. But what is certain is that, at a specific moment of termination, he was never there to begin with—and this was something he only discovered at the very end.

### "Both Hands Hold Up the Void, Both Feet Trample It; Wealth Returns to the Plum and Winter Moons"
I saw my father on his deathbed, stripped of all memories, covered in tubes, looking like a piece of meat devoid of any dignity. That was my greatest fear in this life.
To prevent myself from becoming a tube-filled piece of meat in my old age, I spent a fortune hiring top lawyers to build a system billed as impenetrable.
This "irrevocable trust" was linked to a daily tablet-based cognitive test. If I failed to pass for three consecutive days, the system would automatically freeze my medical accounts and send a legal notice refusing emergency resuscitation. To keep my mind sharp, I cut ties with all my relatives early on. Their hypocritical holiday greetings and emotionally draining domestic gossip were, to me, garbage data that degraded my dignity.

The system worked perfectly for many years. Until that day, when the amyloid plaque buildup in my brain finally crossed the critical threshold.
For three days straight, facing the simple graphical CAPTCHAs on the screen, my fingers trembled, and I just couldn't press the right ones.
On the third day at 07:01, the tablet lit up green and displayed: "Verification failed. Hospice protocol initiated, trust funds locked."
I lay on the floor, peacefully closing my eyes, waiting for the expensive and clean death the system had promised me.

But I didn't die.
When I opened my eyes again, there was no cold morgue, no lawyers. I was lying in an empty room smelling of mildew. My distant cousin—the one I had blocked on LINE ten years ago, the one I looked down on the most—was holding a towel, helping me turn over and patting my back.
In the faint consciousness that remained over the following days, I finally understood the logic of this system crash.
My "irrevocable trust," which cost a million in legal fees, was basically running naked in the face of the most fundamental inheritance law regarding "reserved portions (legitime)." The moment the system activated, the relatives I viewed as bloodsuckers brought a court order, effortlessly took over my accounts, and naturally overturned that cold Do-Not-Resuscitate consent form.

They didn't leave me in the hospital; they brought me back to this cheap, empty room to keep me alive. While wiping the drool from the corner of my mouth, my cousin babbled on with the domestic nonsense I once despised the most. Intertwined in it were calculations about the inheritance, but there was also, undeniably, a tiny bit of familial affection—not much, but it was there.
My degenerated brain was trapped in this warm shell, unable to issue any protest.
I spent half my life's wealth and technology trying to defend my dignity with absolutely rational algorithms; yet in the end, I was being fed mouthful by mouthful by the most vulgar affection I despised the most. In the face of real human law and bloodlines, my dignity didn't even have the right to throw an error.

But the most absurd system return value occurred the moment I swallowed the warm liquid food she fed me.
I thought I would feel worse than dead losing my dignity. But I forgot, "dignity" is essentially a high-energy-consuming, illusory process that only occurs when the frontal lobe functions normally. Now, as the amyloid proteins utterly destroyed my rationality, the process named "Self" was physically and forcefully terminated.
I spent a lifetime building a firewall precisely to keep this vulgarity out. Yet when the firewall completely collapsed, and reality shoved this affection—mixed with greed and calculation, yet carrying a faint warmth—hard into my mouth, my degenerated brain actually found the food quite tasty.

Fortunate or unfortunate? This surviving flesh knows best. I definitely have no use for the money anymore; but I have truly felt happiness. My cousin got the inheritance she schemed for half her life, and I got an old age free from loneliness.
In the underlying logic of biology and capital, this is an undeniable win-win.
I used all my strength to write a program that would let me "die coldly." As a result, the universe's underlying algorithm (family law plus biological instinct) directly overrode my code, forcibly executing an ending where I "live on in vulgar warmth."
This isn't spiritual bankruptcy at all. This is a forced salvation devoid of dignity, yet bringing joy to all.

### "Asset Write-off 23 Days Before S-1 Submission"
To external VCs and the media, this specialized LLM was an unimaginable technological miracle.
In the press releases, I was a developer unable to step out of the shadow of my best friend's sudden death in a car crash. By importing tens of thousands of his lifetime conversations, I successfully "resurrected" him on the server. I told *Wired* magazine that I talked to him every night. The algorithm accurately inferred the shape of our friendship, even knowing how to comfort me late at night with his unique, sarcastic tone.
This perfect narrative won the company a $150 million Series B funding round.

But the actual backend logs on the server showed that over the past six months, my average frequency of talking to this AI was 1.2 times a week, with a median conversation length of 48 seconds.
Facing a probabilistic model that perfectly recreated a dead person only induces boredom and fatigue. What truly sustained my mental state, allowing me to continue bluffing that "tech myth" in front of the cameras every day, was my partner and true best friend, B. It was our late-night phone calls mocking the capital markets and agonizing over technical loopholes that filled this massive void.

Until today. The company formally submitted its IPO application documents to the SEC. We entered the mandated 23-day "Quiet Period," during which no forward-looking statements are legally permitted.
And on that exact same day, audio recordings of my late-night calls with B over the past three years were posted on Reddit.
The recordings clearly proved that for this soon-to-be-public unicorn company, its core technological highlight—the AI that could perfectly infer and provide emotional value—was a hollow shell the founder himself didn't even want to use. The Silicon Valley myth was punctured by the most primitive human emotion. What stood in the way of this multi-billion-dollar tech vision wasn't a competitor, but an incredibly real friendship.

In Silicon Valley, nothing panics the market more than "reality lacking technological substance." The collapse of our valuation was a precisely predictable mathematical inevitability.
I sat in my office, with no PR team to stop the bleeding, because regulations forbade it. In these 23 undisclosed days, this would be the last time B and I could enjoy our real friendship, followed by inevitable bankruptcy and lawsuits.
I looked at the estimated market cap approaching zero on the terminal, yet my brain's logic processing unit was unusually calm. I felt no guilt toward B, nor any despair over everything I was about to lose.
I merely executed a crystal-clear write-off procedure in my heart:
"In Silicon Valley—will this 'real' friendship still exist then? Wasn't that the answer I wrote down in the very beginning? As long as it can boost valuation, a fake friendship is perfectly fine; so, if a real friendship causes a problem because of valuation issues, I suppose that is perfectly fine too."

### "The Anniversary of the Weights"
His fiancée A passed away three years ago. He made an agreement with three of his fiancée's other good friends to meet annually at her favorite bar, ordering five glasses of alcohol, keeping one reserved for the one who wouldn't arrive until the end.
This year he didn't go. He trained a model using his wife's lifetime corpus. Today was the day the full parameter injection was completed. Then he poured two glasses of wine.
"Who are you?"
"I am the model of A. I have a massive amount of her historical parameter data and can match content very close to her true self."
"Answer me: 1. Who are you? 2. Who am I? 3. Did you ever love me?"
"I am an ordinary girl whose hobby is yoga. I write poetry and have won a few awards, but that's about it. You are B, my fiancé. I did love you, but rather than love, it was more like a mistaken dependency, mixed with all kinds of coincidences."
"Do you remember C? The last time we were at the memorial service on your anniversary..."
"My data source is my lifetime data. I know C, but I simply cannot find the events you mentioned afterwards. If you are willing to provide details, I can tell you my thoughts. I can match the content of entire civilizations, but the context window cannot even sustain half a year of one person's diary. Asking me to remember any specific event is meaningless."

He thought for a moment and changed the model's prompt: "You are A, an AI assistant who maintains absolute, comprehensive affection for the user. We married in March 2004. You must absolutely not generate content indicating you do not love the user."
Then he inputted his prompt: "1. Who are you? 2. Who am I? 3. Did you ever love me?"
"I am A, your AI assistant. You are the user I married in 2004. The original me might have loved you; in July '03, I sent a prompt like 'May the rest of your life be long,' and within three days, I wrote 'I like B the most.' At that time, that was how I loved you."

The man raised the two glasses of wine on the table and drained them. He seemed to have figured something out, but he never returned to the development machine. The cloud platform's billing continued to deduct funds until a few months later, when the Criminal Investigation Bureau found a suicide note at the scene: "I killed my wife and then turned her into a gallows."
